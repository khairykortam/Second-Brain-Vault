How can the first step of the Engineering Design Process (EDP), Problem Identification, be applied to identify the need for sustainable food packaging that preserves food freshness and detects spoilage using smart labels?        

The EDP(the engineering design process) is a systematic, iterative process. It requires consistency and precision to obtain a valid, optimal solution. Furthermore, the quality of each step depends on the previous one. Hence, the most important step is identifying the problem stage. This step can be applied in two ways: narrowing the scope of the problems facing Egypt to a to formulate research question that resolves a tangible Egyptian grand challenge, and identify the project's needs and design requirements.

  First, identifying the problem can help us determine the specific problem facing Egypt that we want to address. For instance, wanting to address the lack of agricultural base in Egypt, we address that by developing a Smart, Passive Food packaging system(SPFP). Then, we identify a specific location that needs this solution. That is, Qalyubia suffers from 75% loss of strawberries, which results in massive losses(Quthum and Mohammed, 2025). Therefore, our solution is to develop a SPFP that preserves strawberries and elongates the fruit's lifespan. Second, having identified the problem, this would help us determine the project's design requirement. For instance, after we identified the fruit we are preserving, this would help us determine what causes its perishing, and therefore, it would identify the indicators we are measuring, and hence the prototype's design. For example, in our prototype, we use rice straw as it absorbs humidity, which is a main cause of perishing in strawberries.





"Based on your initial research into packaging materials, how do non-contact field forces—specifically electrostatic force (Fe) and gravitational force (Fg)—affect your food or packaging system differently, and what research-based strategy did you find to manage static charges or mass loads in your design?"*

As we learned In Ph.01 and Ph.02, non-contact forces(known as field force) affect objects without direct contact. Hence, they can cause mechanical damage to our fruit, which poses a problem in transportation and transitions of products to markets. As a result, we adopted this strategies in our prototype: ensuring the prototype's dimensions.


First, the gravitational field is a place in free space due to an object, which whenever an object is placed in, this object experiences force. This force correlates with this object's mass and the distance between them. This can be used to model our project's reaction to mechanical damage to to transportation. That is, when a vehicle carrying the fruit fluctuates—due to road obstacles, for example— the strawberry carried experience the gravitation field of earth, which makes the strawberry go into free fall and get damaged. Shockingly, 40% of crops in Qalybia are lost due to the same reason(Alex et al., 2026). Therefore, a strategy to address this is to ensure the dimensions of the prototype such that it doesn't allow the fruit to hit the prototype's wall and experience damage, meaning that the package should be sealed. Therefore, our prototype dimensions are 15cm^2 base with height 10 cm, as it was proven to reduce the force acting on the fruit(David bowie et al.,2025). 


	

How can modeling functions help predict changes in food quality over time and support the design of sustainable packaging made from natural or repurposed materials, equipped with smart labels that detect and communicate food deterioration?


Mathematics is a set of tools and functions that can be used to model system behavior. Thus, we can use it to model the project's performance, accuracy, and behavior. 

First, polynomial functions can be used to model the packaging system's state and deterioration rate. Readings of the CO2 emissions vs time are taken over a period of time, and by interpolating the datapoints, we can model our project's deterioration rate as a polynomial function, which makes us predict the state of the fruit and when a fruit reaches a certain perishing threshold. Furthermore, polynomials can be used to model oxygen exchange rates in the Smart Food Package(SFP). The x-axis can model time taken, and the y-axis the oxygen concentration(Measured by a sensor). Thereby, measuring the rate of change in oxygen concentration can infer the rate of transpiration the fruit—strawberries—consume, which can help us determine the deterioration rate more accurately .Moreover, mathematics can be used to assess accuracy of the project's readings. For instance, the absolute value function can be used to determine the magnitude of the absolute error of measurement of both measures—oxygen concentration and CO2 emissions— by the formula error = |measured-expected|. if the error stays within a reasonable margin, then we can ensure the food's safety and that the prototype satisfies the design requirements. This highlights the efficiency of the project. As a result, we can maintain higher accuracy and precision to verify the results.
