Strawberry farming plays a significant role in Egypt's agricultural output and in its international trade. In 2015, the country produced about 428,000 tonnes of strawberries on around 22,000 feddans, and its exports amounted to nearly 39,000 tonnes, which was about 9% of total production (Bahloul, 2017). More recently, in 2023, Egypt exported approximately 23,947 tonnes of fresh strawberries, the value of which was around US$132.4 million, showing that the crop remains an important part of the nation's export economy (World Bank, 2023). It is essential to maintain the quality of these products since even a relatively short delay in cooling can speed up deterioration. In an experimental study, Nunes et al. (2005) discovered that rapid cooling reduced the average occurrence of decay in strawberries that had not been inoculated by 25% and also lessened the severity of the decay by about 24%, highlighting the need for proper postharvest handling.

The Qalyubia Governorate is a suitable place to study this issue since research has recorded commercial strawberry farming and the agricultural methods used in the area (Bahloul, 2017)(Hassan et al., 2018). That said, the strawberries grown here can still encounter postharvest problems linked to fungal infection, mechanical damage, temperature variations, and poor moisture regulation. Botrytis cinerea is capable of appearing even when the strawberries are stored at refrigerated temperatures, and inappropriate packaging together with prolonged moisture accumulation can also raise the risk of decay (Petrasch et al., 2019)(Nunes et al., 2005). Even though better cooling and packaging technologies can assist in maintaining fruit quality, their effectiveness relies on the appropriate choice of materials and the storage conditions (Priyadarshi et al., 2024).

The particular problem that this project is aiming to solve is the postharvest deterioration of strawberries in Qalyubia as a result of gray mold and poor moisture regulation during storage and transportation, since this can endanger product quality, lead to rejection when exporting, and result in greater agricultural waste. There is still a need for local data on the incidence of mold and on the rates of export rejection in order to determine the full extent of this problem in Qalyubia.**
