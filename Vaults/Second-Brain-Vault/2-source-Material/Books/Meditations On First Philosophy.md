# Meditations on First Philosophy 

## First Meditation: On what can be called into doubt

To lie a solid foundation, Descartes' methodology is, instead of doubting each belief on it own, to reject anything that at least have *some* reason to doubt. For instance, a structures falls apart when its foundations are demolished.

Descartes asserts that senses might deceive us, but maybe not all the time. Then, he expounds that our dreams also deceive us, and that we sometimes mistake dreams for reality. 

Descartes divides objects into two categories: general, which includes body, extension(shape), quantity, and abstract—geometry, arithmetic, and disciplines of this sort— that are detached from reality. Then, Descartes presents the infamous "evil demon" that deceives you at every turn, rather than begin done by an omnibenovelent, morally perfect God.

What remains true? Descartes argues that this demon, though all powerful, cannot convince you of being nothing while you think you are something.

I realized that if I wanted to establish anything in the sciences that was stable and likely to last, I needed—just once in my life—to demolish everything completely and start again from the foundations.
