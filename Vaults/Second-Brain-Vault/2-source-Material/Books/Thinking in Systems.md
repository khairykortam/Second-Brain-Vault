# Thinking in Systems

# PART ONE: System Structure and Behavior

## —ONE— The Basics

A system consists of a set of things, interconnected such that they produce certain behaviors and patterns overtime. Not only that, but they also achieve a goal. Hence, a system is more than the sum of its parts. Elements do not need to be physics, they can be metaphysical—such as information, emotions, etc. So is interconnections. Interconnections can be represented by a flow of information within the system. The hardest crucial part of a system is it's purpose; you need to watch the system and deduce it's purpose. Interestingly enough, the purpose of each subsystem may add up to an undesired behavior.

System components are the following: stocks(measurable quantities), a flow, inflow and out flow rate, and feedback(including balancing feedback and reinforcement loops).
