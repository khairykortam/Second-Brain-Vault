## One-line takeaway
- Def: Philosophy is simply pondering and reflecting about some questions and their interrelations, learning what has already been said about them and why; we're all philosophers to some extend.
- testimony can equal, but can't surpass, rationality in matters of unnatural events, argued Hume.
- multiple people observing the same thing though they're unrelated can have the same purpose as replicability in scientific matters, as miracles and historical, unlike scientific experiments, can't be "rerun" or replicated 
- buddhist "five aggregates"(what constitutes humans): material form, feeling, perception, mental formations, and consciousness.
- A person *cannot* be identified with any of them.
- We aren't *those* tendencies, but rather what *has* those tendencies
- material alone cannot act on its mind - so a body needs something to direct it.(argued decartes)

- Some themes:
- ethical consequetialism: technical jargon for that principle that how good or bad something is is to be judged by it's consequences; in contrast to Socrates view in Crito.
- A problem is that consequenctialism says good consequences are good because they are good; thus, establishing axioms about somethings as being good in themselves. 
- For instance, you can suggest more than value as an axiomatic value, but they may conflict, complicating matter further, some philosophers argue.
- Epicurus: minimizing pain(which is what he means by pleasure, meaning the ideal life is one where pain in minimized, which is the highest form of pleasure(stable))
- John Mill(Utilitariansim), in addition to epicurus view, he adds the aim to improve life for *everybody*, elaborating that you're own happiness is a goal important just as much as anyone else's.(in short, maximizing total happiness regardless the benefited)
- Integrity: "life lived as a whole rather than as a series of disconnected episodes"
