- types of writing: narrative, academic, creative, argumentative, expository, descriptive
- narrative writing: 
- setting-> place,time,condition, atmosphere.-> creates, mode, without it, the story feels empty. peaceful village, battlefield, ...
-types of setting details: visuals, sound, smell, weather.
- characters-. experience the conflict, make decisions, drive the action
-types of character: protagonist, supporting characters, antagonist(can be the main character(anti-hero))
-character development: goals, developed through actions, not just descriptions. 
- conflict-> purpose, problem, struggle, drive.
- types of conflicts: man vs. nature, society, self*, man, creatures
-
- reflection/ resolution->
- resolution: what happens after the main conflict
- reflection: the narrator's interpretation of the experience

- types of narrative point of view: first/third-person
- first-person: pronouns: I,we, me, my, very personal/emotional, reader experiences events through the narrator, narrator only knows what they see, hear, and feel, you can impersonate another person
- third-person: outside the story, but follows one character's thoughts and experiences.: they, he, she
- first-person observer


- academic writing: "here is the idea, and here is the evidence." believe and understand
- it is formal, it uses facts, examples, and research, it is clear and organized, personal opinions must be supported.
- objectivity: facts
- evidence-based arguments: statistics, research findings, real-world examples.

- Creative Writing: rather than ideas, and facts, creative writing wants the reader to feel something. Therefore, it could use imagination, fictional quotes and other.
- it uses imagination, it focuses on experience and feeling, it plays with language and emotion, it could connect fiction to reality.
- figurative language: simile(as/like)-> moving like shadows, metaphor, personification(of objects)  
- showing vs telling, show rather than tell, their hands trembled, rather than they were scared.


- Argumentative writing: a lawyer, want to convince someone that you opinion or idea is right
- claim-> evidence-> facts, examples, handle opposition by acknowledging counterarguments.
- expository vs descriptive:
- both give something to the reader, but expository explain or teach something , showing facts and ideas, while descriptive uses words to paint a picture for the reader to help the reader see those words
- expository focuses on facts and information with clear explanation, while descriptive focuses on feelings and imagining, full sensory
