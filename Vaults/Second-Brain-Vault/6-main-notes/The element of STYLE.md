## One-line takeaway

1. Form the possessive singular of nouns by adding 's.
2. In a series of three or more terms with a single conjunction, use a comma after each term except the last
3. Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas: -dates: wed, Nov 14, 1999, -a name or a title is parenthetic.:
 etc, i.e, eg, and abbreviation for academic degrees, and titles that follow a name are parenthetic.
 commas shouldn't sperate a noun from a restrictive term of identification(e.g Jr, billy the kid, x the y.)
  People sitting in the rear couldn't hear.
 4. Place a comma before a conjunction introducing an independent clause.
 5.  Do not join independent clauses with a comma
 exception is when the two clauses are very short and alike in form, to use a comma instead of a simicolon: May proposes, God disposes....
 6. Do not break sentences in two(i.e, do not use periods for commas.)
 7. Use a colon after an independent clause to introduce a list of particulars, an appositive, an amplification, or an illustrative quotation
 8. Use a dash to set off an abrupt break or interruption and to announce a long appositive or summary
 use a dash only when a common mark seems inadequate.
 9. The number of the subject determines the number of the verb
 10. Use the proper case of pronoun
 11. A participal phrase at the biginning of a sentence must refer to the grammatical subject
 12. Choose a suitable design and hold to it
 13. Make the paragraph the unit of composition
 Each line in a diaglouge is a paragraph.
 14. use the active voice
 To make things lively, use expressions as there is. or could be heard...
 15. put statements in positive form.
 Negative words other than not are usually strong
  16. Use definite, specific, concrete language(vivid language)
  17. Omit needless words( don't use the "fact that","who is", "which has", etc....)
  18. Avoid a succession of loose sentences(especially those consistening of two clauses, the second introduced by a conjuction or relative)
  19. Express coordinate ideas in similar form
  20. Keep related words together
  The subject and verb should not, as a rule, be separated by a phrase or clause that can be transferred to the beginning
  21. In summaries, keep to one tense
  In summarizing the action of a drama, a poem, a story, or a novel, use the present
  22. Place the emphatic words of  a sentence at the end.

 - A Few Matters of Form:
 - colloquialisms : if you were to use them, just do that.
 - Excalmations: don't attempt to empahsize simple statements by using them.
 - hyphen: when two or more words are combined to form a compound adjective.
 - Don't spell out dates or serial numbers, except in dialogue
 - parentheses: انا كسم اباجورة
 - when to indent and not include quotation?
 - quotations introduced by that don't have ""
 - proverbial expressions and familiar pharases of literary origin require no ""

- Words and Expressions commonly Misued:
- Aggravate: "to add to" an already troublesome 
- Irritate: "to annoy"
- allude: indirect mention
- Allusion: not illusion
- between: when more than 2 are but each is considered individually
- and/or -> confusing, maybe use x or y or both or something of that manner.
- anticipate: use expect in the sense of simple expectation
- as good or better than -> rearranged: as good as his, or better.
- as to whether -> whether is sufficient
- as yet -> use only yet.
- being: not appropriate after regard... as.
- but: unneccesary after doubt and help. Don't overuse it.
- can: means "to be able" not to be used as a subsitute for may.
- case: often unneccessary
- Certainly: don't abuse it
- Character: often simply redundant
- Claim: not a substitute for declare, maintain, or charge
- Clever: when used with hourse, it means good-natured
- Compare: compared to: resemblence. Compared with: differences
- comprise: Literally, "embrace"
- Consider: Not followed by as, except if it means "examined" or "discussed"
- Currently: often redundant and imprecise
- Different than: wrong. It's "different *from*"
- Disinterested: Means "impartial"
- Due to: Loosely used for through, because of. However, in correct use, it's synonymous with "attributable to"
-  Each and every one: avoid, except in dialogue
- Effect: as a noun, means "result". as a verb, means "to bring about", "to accomplish"(not affect, which mean "to influence")
- Enormity: means "monstrous wickedness" not size
- Enthuse: not recommended
- Etc: Literally: "and other things", eqv to "and the rest", "and so forth". misfit in formal wirting. wrong in the end of a list introduced by "such as", "for example"
- Fact: Verfications, not judgements
- Facility: why must jails, hospitals, schools suddenly becom "facilities"?
- Factor, Feature: just don't.
- Finalize; ambigous
- Folk: only singular when used for "people"
- have got: don't use in writing
- Gratuitous: unearned/undeserved
- he is a man who : don't
- However: avoid starting a sentence with however when the meaning is "nevertheless". The words usually seves better when not in first position
- infer and imply: not interchangable
- Importantly :avoid
- in regard to = as regards
- In the last analysis -> bankrupt
- In terms of : better ommited
- Interesting: just don't
- Irregardless: should be regardless
- be sus of -ize
- kind of: restricted to the literal sense
- Leave: don't misuse for let
- Less: don't misuse for fewer
- Like: not to be used for oncjunction as. it governs nouns and pronouns
- Literal, literally: don't use except in the literal sense, not for exaggeration.
- Meaningful: meh
- Nature: meh
- Nor: used wrongly for or after negative expressions
- Nouns as verbs: suspect
- one of the most: meh
- partially: "to some extend". Partly: "some part"
- Relate: not to be used intransitively to suggest rapport.
- respectively: try omitting 
- secondly, thirdly, etc.: second, third, etc is preferable
- avoid So as an intensifier
- sort of: don't
- try avoid split infinitive
- state: not substittue for say, remark. Restrict to "express fully or clearly"
- than: make sure nothing is missing
- dont' use They when the antecdent is each, everbydon, etc... USe the singular pronoun. he for both genders is fine, or use they instead, or drop it all together
- This: avoid ambiguity.
- while: don't susbsitute for and,but. when you don't know, just put a semicolon. 
- -wise: just don't
- when transitioning from general habit to sepcific events, explicity indicate it to avoid confusion.
- (my note): even though -> just use though
